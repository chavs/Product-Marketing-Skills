# Voice Calibration

Research method for a deliverable that must sound like a specific company or medium: a blog post, an email, a LinkedIn post, a one-pager. It finds real samples and measures them. It does not write the deliverable. The candidate writes it.

## Steps

1. **Name the target medium.** Match the deliverable's medium, not the company's best-known one. A blog assignment is calibrated on the company's blog, not its homepage. An email assignment is calibrated on real customer or product emails, if any are public.
2. **Pull 3 to 5 samples from that medium.** Prefer recent ones (last 12 months) in the company's own voice, not guest posts, unless the brief says otherwise. Record the URL and date of each.
3. **Measure, do not describe.** For each sample, note rough average sentence length, person (we, you, they), tone (plain, technical, playful), use of headings and lists, jargon level, how it opens and closes, and length in words. Report ranges, not impressions.
4. **State the pattern and the outliers.** What holds across most samples, and what one sample does differently.
5. **Flag gaps.** If fewer than 3 samples of the right medium exist, say so and name the closest substitute medium. Never fill the gap with a different medium without saying so.

## Source-trust ranking

1. The company's own published pieces in the target medium
2. The company's own pieces in a neighboring medium, flagged as a substitute
3. Third-party commentary on the company's voice, flagged as weak

## Tagging

`[Confirmed]` for a measurement taken from a sample read in full. `[Estimated]` for a measurement from a partial read. Put the source link on every sample.

## Out of scope

Writing the deliverable, or rewriting the candidate's draft to match the voice. In Phase D the draft can be compared with these measurements and mismatches flagged. The fix is the candidate's.
