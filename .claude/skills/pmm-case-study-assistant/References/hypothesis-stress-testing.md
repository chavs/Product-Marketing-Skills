# Hypothesis Stress-Testing

When the candidate shares an existing working hypothesis (per Phase A's "existing point of view" question), the default research tracks tend to surface confirming evidence more readily than disconfirming evidence — a track researching "does X differentiate DataDome" naturally finds material framed around X, not material arguing X is wrong. That's a structural bias, not a one-off mistake, and it needs its own explicit countermeasure rather than trusting the regular tracks to catch it incidentally.

## When to run this as its own track

Any time the candidate states a hypothesis with more than one distinct claim/plank in it, run this as a dedicated track alongside (not instead of) the normal research tracks — don't fold falsification into a track that's also trying to build the confirming case, since the same researcher/pass tends to under-weight whichever direction it isn't primarily looking for.

## Method

1. **Split the hypothesis into its individual planks.** A compound hypothesis ("X is true, and Y is true, and Z follows from both") needs each plank tested independently — one plank holding up doesn't mean they all do, and a candidate should know exactly which planks are solid vs. shaky.
2. **For each plank, search specifically for disconfirming evidence, not neutral evidence.** Adversarial query framing helps here — search for the strongest counter-example, the competitor or analyst who'd argue the opposite, the data point that doesn't fit — rather than searching the plank's own terms and taking whatever comes back. If the plank claims "no clear leader exists yet," specifically search for "who is the leader" and "who was named a leader" rather than just "market landscape for X."
3. **Report both sides side by side, evidence-first, verdict-last.** For each plank: the strongest evidence found FOR it, the strongest evidence found AGAINST it, then a plain verdict (holds / partially holds / doesn't hold / genuinely unresolved) — the verdict has to follow visibly from the evidence shown, not be asserted ahead of it.
4. **A plank that "doesn't hold" is a valid, useful outcome — not a failure of the research.** The point of this track is to prevent the candidate from building an answer on a plank that would collapse under an interviewer's first follow-up question. Finding that out now is the win condition, not an inconvenience to soften.

## Tagging

Same `[Confirmed]` / `[Estimated]` / `[Inferred]` discipline as every other track, applied to both the confirming and disconfirming evidence equally — don't hold disconfirming evidence to a stricter sourcing bar than confirming evidence, or the stress-test just reintroduces the bias it exists to remove.

## Out of scope

Deciding what the candidate should do about a plank that doesn't hold (drop it, reframe it, hedge it in the write-up) — that's the candidate's judgment call once they see the evidence, not this track's.
