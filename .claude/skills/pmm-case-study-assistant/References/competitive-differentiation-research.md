# Competitive Differentiation (User-Oriented Roadmap) Research

Research method for a case-study task shaped like: "analyze us vs. named competitors (G2 + website), draw the main differentiators, and recommend a roadmap to further stand out." This is the same underlying discipline as `live-job-interview-assistant/References/live-competitive-research.md`, run without the live-interview time pressure — thoroughness is the point here, not speed, since this executes unattended.

## Steps

1. **Resolve each named competitor to the relevant product/product-line** — the same company may have multiple products; research the one that actually competes in the problem space the brief names.
2. **Per competitor, gather:**
   - G2 review-mined themes — both praise and recurring complaints. Note approximate volume and recency of complaints, not just their existence; one old, isolated complaint isn't the same finding as a pattern across many recent reviews.
   - Website/solution-page positioning and feature claims — how the competitor describes itself, in its own words, not how anyone else frames it.
   - Whether and how the competitor already covers the specific expansion area the brief names (e.g. a new use-case category the target company is entering) — flag which competitors already have a foothold there and which don't. That gap is often exactly where the "how to further differentiate" recommendation should live, even though drafting the recommendation itself is the candidate's job, not this research track's.
3. **Cross-check against the Track 0 value-prop/positioning baseline** (`value-prop-positioning-baseline.md`) — differentiation only means something relative to what the target company itself claims; note explicitly where a competitor's strength lines up against (or exposes a gap in) the target company's own stated positioning.

## Source-trust ranking

Reuses the ranking from `live-job-interview-assistant/References/live-competitive-research.md` without redefining it:

1. The competitor's own documentation, pricing, and architecture pages
2. The target company's own comparison pages about the competitor — read as advocacy, not neutral fact
3. Practitioner-level content — engineering blogs, conference talks from people who've actually used the product
4. Independent analyst sources — Gartner, Forrester, IDC, G2

**Reddit and Hacker News are excluded entirely.**

## Tagging

`[Confirmed]` / `[Estimated]` / `[Inferred]`, inline source link, every claim, no exceptions. Since this track runs unattended and untimed, there's no search-pass cap the way the live version has — go deep — but still state a gap honestly if a specific claim can't be verified rather than filling it with a weak match.

## Out of scope

Drafting the actual "roadmap to further stand out" recommendation — this file covers the research substrate only. That call belongs to the candidate.
