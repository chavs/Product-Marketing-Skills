# Content/Messaging Review Research

Research method for a case-study task shaped like: "review and give feedback on these named pages/assets." The brief is asking for the candidate's own critique — this track pulls raw material for that critique; it does not pre-write the critique or score the pages itself.

## Steps

1. **For each named page/asset, pull the actual copy and structure**: headline, sub-heads, proof points, CTA wording and placement, and any notable structural choices (e.g. a demo video's length/format, a page's information order). Work from the real content, not a guess at what a page "probably" says.
2. **Pull 1–2 comparable competitor pages as an implicit benchmark** — not to score them, just so the candidate has a comparison point when forming their own critique (e.g. "how does a competitor's equivalent product page structure its proof points").
3. **Cross-check every page against the Track 0 baseline** (`value-prop-positioning-baseline.md`) — flag, don't resolve, any place a page's messaging seems inconsistent with the company's own stated positioning elsewhere. That inconsistency might be exactly the feedback worth giving; surfacing it is this track's job, judging it is the candidate's.
4. **Provide a short, generic content-critique checklist** as reference material the candidate can apply themselves:
   - Value-prop clarity — is the core claim stated plainly near the top, or buried/implied?
   - Proof-point specificity — concrete evidence (numbers, named customers, named integrations) vs. vague claims
   - CTA strength and placement — is the next step obvious and proportionate to the page's intent?
   - Jargon load — would the stated ICP actually recognize this language?
   - Internal consistency — does this page's message match the Track 0 baseline and the other pages under review?

## Source-trust ranking

The pages/assets named in the brief are the primary source by definition — this track is about accurately capturing what's actually there, not ranking sources. For the optional competitor-benchmark pages, use the same ranking as `competitive-differentiation-research.md` (competitor's own pages first).

## Tagging

Direct pulls from the named pages don't need `[Confirmed]`/`[Estimated]` tagging (they're primary sources, quoted or closely paraphrased with a link). Any claim *about* those pages that goes beyond what's directly stated — an inference about intent, an inconsistency flag — should be tagged and sourced like any other claim.

## Out of scope

Writing the actual feedback/critique of the pages — this file covers the raw material (copy, structure, benchmark, checklist) only. The candidate's own judgment is the deliverable the brief is actually asking for.
