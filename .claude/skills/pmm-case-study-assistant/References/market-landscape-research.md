# Market-Landscape (Analyst-Report-Driven) Roadmap Research

Research method for a case-study task shaped like: "given this analyst report placement, propose ONE product-related project to maintain or extend our position." Generic across report types (Forrester Wave, Gartner Magic Quadrant, IDC MarketScape, G2 Grid, etc.) — don't hardcode a specific report or company here.

## Steps

1. **Read the actual report, not just the excerpt quoted in the brief.** Briefs typically quote a favorable snippet; the full report has the methodology, the specific criteria being scored, and — if a prior edition exists — how the company's placement changed edition over edition. A placement described only by the brief's excerpt is incomplete: find what axis the company leads or lags on, and exactly which criteria drive that axis.
2. **Check the company's own recent public roadmap signals** — blog, changelog, press releases, recent product-launch pages — before treating any candidate project as novel. A recommendation that turns out to already be shipped or already announced is a real credibility miss.
3. **Gather industry-trend context that would justify timing a bet now** — an emerging threat/problem category, a shift in buyer behavior, a new regulation — sourced from actual reporting or analyst commentary, not speculation. This is what makes "why now" defensible rather than arbitrary.
4. **If the brief asks about internal advocacy** (e.g. "how would you sell this to the product team"), separately gather *patterns* for how PMMs typically build product-team buy-in: evidence/data framing, competitive-urgency framing, prioritization frameworks (RICE, ICE, etc.) as vocabulary and structure — raw material for the candidate's own pitch, not a drafted pitch itself.

## Source-trust ranking

1. The analyst report's own text (full report, not just an excerpt)
2. The company's own roadmap/press/product-launch signals
3. Independent industry-trend journalism or practitioner analysis of the problem space
4. General trend blogs / secondary commentary on the report

## Tagging

`[Confirmed]` / `[Estimated]` / `[Inferred]`, inline source link, every claim, no exceptions.

## Out of scope

Drafting the actual project recommendation, launch plan, success metrics, or advocacy pitch — this file only covers what gets researched, not what gets decided. That judgment call belongs to the candidate (see `../SKILL.md`'s core principle).
