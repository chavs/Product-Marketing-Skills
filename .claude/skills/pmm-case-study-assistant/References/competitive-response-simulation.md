# Competitive Response Simulation

A supplementary research angle for a competitive/roadmap task: not just "what are the differentiators" (see `competitive-differentiation-research.md`), but "if the target company stated this specific position publicly, how would each named competitor push back on it, and with what." Useful because a roadmap or differentiation answer that doesn't survive an obvious competitor counter-argument is weaker than one that's already been pressure-tested against it.

## Ground rules

- **Never fabricate a quote or attribute invented words to a real company.** Everything here is either (a) something the competitor has actually published (a comparison page, a blog post, launch messaging, a G2 response to a review) that functions as a rebuttal, or (b) an explicitly labeled synthesis of how their *own established, real positioning* would likely apply to this specific claim — never presented as if it were a direct statement.
- **Tag every constructed (non-quoted) response** `[Inferred — synthesized from the competitor's own established positioning, not a direct statement]`, distinct from the normal `[Inferred]` tag used elsewhere, so the candidate can immediately see which responses are sourced rebuttals vs. reasoned extrapolation.
- **Check for direct rebuttal material first**, before synthesizing anything: does the competitor have a named "vs. [target company]" comparison page, a battlecard-style content piece, or a G2 answer that already argues against a claim like this one? Direct material always outranks synthesis.

## Method

For each named competitor, and for each distinct position/claim the target company stakes out in a given task:

1. **State the target company's claim plainly** (one sentence) — this is the thing being pressure-tested, not something to research further here.
2. **Check for a direct rebuttal**: named comparison pages, competitive-positioning blog content, or review-site responses that already argue against this kind of claim. Use it directly if found, quoted/sourced like any other claim.
3. **If no direct rebuttal exists, synthesize the most plausible pushback from the competitor's own real, established positioning** — their actual value prop, their actual go-to-market angle, their actual product gaps or strengths already documented in `competitive-differentiation-research.md`. Ask: given how this competitor already talks about itself and its rivals, what's the strongest, most in-character objection they'd raise? Tag it as synthesized, per the ground rules above.
4. **Note where a competitor has no credible pushback available** — this is itself a finding, and arguably the more useful one: a claim that survives this pass because the competitor's own public material has nothing to counter it with is stronger evidence than a claim nobody tried to break.

## Out of scope

Deciding how the candidate should pre-empt or address a strong competitive rebuttal in their own answer — that's their judgment call. This file only covers surfacing the rebuttal.
