# Customer Voice Research

A method for surfacing how real customers/buyers actually talk about a problem — used live, mid-interview, for two distinct purposes that both matter equally here:

1. **Language calibration** — how does a persona actually describe this pain, so the candidate's own phrasing (a value prop, a headline, an answer to a question) sounds like it came from talking to a real customer, not from generic marketing copy.
2. **Fact-checking** — verifying a specific claim about what a customer experienced, said, or achieved, when that claim is about to be used as evidence.

Both are first-class. Don't treat one as the "real" use case and the other as incidental.

## Scope

This isn't tied to a single vendor. It covers whichever is relevant to the moment: how a *target company's own* customers talk about their experience, how a *competitor's* customers talk about theirs, or — just as often, especially for language calibration during something like the product-to-value translation test — how a persona talks about the *problem itself*, independent of which vendor they ended up with. Use whichever framing the question actually calls for; don't force every lookup into a single-vendor shape.

## Source-trust ranking

This ranking governs any claim about what a customer actually experienced, said, or achieved — distinct from the general product-fact ranking in `References/live-competitive-research.md`.

1. **The company's own published case studies, customer videos, and customer quotes** — openly curated/biased, but the bias is transparent and known, unlike incentivized or gamed reviews. Best source for specific real customer language and outcomes. Star ratings and sentiment scores from review sites are excluded from this tier — they're ranked separately, below.
2. **Practitioner talks and written content from non-vendor industry voices** — people in the industry discussing the problem space and how they solved it, not vendor-produced content.
3. **Analyst review sites with real customer input** — G2, Gartner Peer Insights. Ranked lowest because reviews can be incentivized or gamed; use for directional color, not as the primary source for a claim.

**Reddit and Hacker News are excluded entirely — not deprioritized, not used at any tier.**

Why case studies outrank review sites despite both being non-neutral: a case study is openly curated, and everyone reading it knows that. Review-site sentiment can be quietly manipulated — incentivized reviews, vendor-gamed review drives, unverifiable reviewer identity — which is a different and worse kind of bias than transparent curation.

## Tagging

Every customer-voice claim gets the same `[Confirmed]` / `[Estimated]` / `[Inferred]` tagging and inline sourcing as product-fact claims. This applies whether the goal is calibrating phrasing or verifying a fact — a claim used as evidence needs the tag even if it's only feeding word choice.

## Live speed

There's usually no time to do a deep language-research pass mid-sentence. What's useful live is fast, targeted retrieval — a handful of real phrases or one verified claim, not an exhaustive survey. Answer what's asked; don't try to build a full phrasing library on the spot.
